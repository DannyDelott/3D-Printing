Sized for one component

Separate rail and stile setups, each with a 3″ margin at the base around the component envelope. The rail uses a short bridge; the stile uses a narrow bridge along its length. Both reproduce the same door radius.

Confirm router fit: the compact bridge has a 114 mm clear lane. The displayed factory base is a 110 mm envelope, not a measured Ryobi. Cutter projection is assumed to be 25.4 mm below the factory base; the floor is 6.35 mm thick.

The wood component comes directly from R7. Fixture dimensions are proposed. Rough stock, tenons, clamp positions and physical stiffness still need checking.

Prints and fit checks

PLA · 0.20 mm layers · 6 walls · 35% gyroid · supplied orientations. Print one guide coupon, one foot and one roller coupon first. Parts fit individually inside a 180 × 180 mm bed; your printer model is unconfirmed.

Wood parts and hardware

Shared hardware: four 608 bearings (22 × 8 × 7 mm), four M8 × 35 mm axles with locking nuts, and eight 0.5 mm steel shims that contact only the inner races. Use eight M5 countersunk bolts to attach the two trucks beneath the bridge; keep heads flush. Guide feet take M5 × 40 mm retaining bolts and wood screws through their 4.5 mm flange holes.

Place the guide backers immediately beyond the component ends, then clamp both to a common base. Bolt every guide section into two feet. Rail guides use six locating pins across the two sets of seams. The short stile guides are single pieces. Screw the end stops into the backers as shown; the stile uses a taller stop on the high side.

Use the R2 feet and trucks with these R2 guides. The narrower roller spacing changes the required guide curve. R1 guides and trucks are not interchangeable with R2.

Bit, setup and cutting sequence

Proposed bit: Freud 16-104 / 16-10425P mortising bit, nominal ¾″ diameter, ¼″ shank and ½″ cutting height. This is a small handheld-router cutter, not a large slab-flattening head. Check the bit’s speed rating and your exact router manual.

  1. With the battery removed, check the factory base fits the lane and can travel without the body, handles or dust attachment hitting the bridge. The depicted router is only a clearance envelope.
  2. Check 25.4 mm projection from factory base to cutter tip with proper collet insertion. Use the supplied gauge only with the battery removed. Do not extend the shank beyond its permitted insertion to meet this dimension. Different projection or floor thickness requires regenerated guide profiles.
  3. Set a rail blank’s flat back 50.8 mm above the guide-backer top plane at the final cut setting. For a stile, use 53.975 mm above that plane; this preserves the ⅛″ face setback. Center each component in its own fixture. The stile guides already contain the correct off-center part of the door curve: place the stile’s inner/grooved edge toward the higher end of the guide. Orient the opposite stile end-for-end to put its inner edge on the same high side while machining. Secure the blank mechanically to a carrier with clamps or sacrificial tabs clear of the full cutter and bridge sweep. The displayed carrier is an envelope; workholding is not finalized.
  4. Raise both guide backers equally for roughing, then lower them together in shallow stages. The bridge floor also needs clearance over the uncut stock; changing bit depth alone does not provide it. Check the full travel with the battery removed at every setup.
  5. Limit each cut according to the cutter, router and wood response. Keep both trucks fully seated; never cut off the guide ends or past the stops. Start and stop with the cutter clear of the stock. Rehearse a small offcut before a door component.
  6. Use closely spaced overlapping passes, followed by light scraping or sanding of the small flats. Measure the result against the door radius. Mill joinery from the flat back before sculpting the face. These jigs cover the rails and stiles. The veneered panel core requires its own thickness allowance and fixture setup.

Reference manual consulted: Ryobi PBLRR01. Your exact ONE+ brushless model still needs confirmation.
