Assembly and hardware

Use straight wooden backers under both guides and a glued, stiff U-section wooden bridge. Printed guides establish the shape; the wood carries the long spans.

Align the four numbered segments from left to right on each backer. Alignment pins only locate the seams; bolt every segment into its feet. Screw feet and travel stops to the backers. Leave no step at a track seam. Bearing spacers must contact only the inner race; verify they do not rub the seals. Mount the trucks beneath the bridge with flush countersunk screw heads in the router lane. Clamp both backers to a common flat base at identical heights.

Bit, setup and cutting sequence

Proposed bit: Freud 16-104 / 16-10425P mortising bit, nominal ¾″ diameter, ¼″ shank and ½″ cutting height. This is a small handheld-router cutter, not a large slab-flattening head. Check the bit’s speed rating and your exact router manual.

  1. With the battery removed, check the factory base fits the lane and can travel without the body, handles or dust attachment hitting the bridge. The depicted router is only a clearance envelope.
  2. Check 25.4 mm projection from factory base to cutter tip with proper collet insertion. Use the supplied gauge only with the battery removed. Do not extend the shank beyond its permitted insertion to meet this dimension. Different projection or floor thickness requires regenerated guide profiles.
  3. Set the flat back of a rail blank 50.8 mm above the final guide-backer top plane (68.8 mm above the bench with 18 mm backers). A stile needs a 53.975 mm back datum above that same backer-top plane to retain its ⅛″ setback. Secure stock mechanically to a carrier with sacrificial tabs or clamps clear of the entire cutter and bridge sweep. Do not hold it by hand.
  4. Raise both guide backers equally for roughing, then lower them together in shallow stages. The bridge floor also needs clearance over the uncut stock; changing bit depth alone does not provide it. Check the full travel with the battery removed at every setup.
  5. Limit each cut according to the cutter, router and wood response. Keep both trucks fully seated; never cut off the guide ends or past the stops. Start and stop with the cutter clear of the stock. Rehearse a small offcut before a door component.
  6. Use closely spaced overlapping passes, followed by light scraping or sanding of the small flats. Measure the result against the door radius. Mill joinery from the flat back before sculpting the face. The panel core needs a separate veneer-thickness allowance; this rail setup is not a finished panel layup specification.
